Previous surveys, for instance \cite{wang2025embodied}, organize prior work into discrete taxonomy categories.
However, such structuring is often insufficient as they overlook the inherent choices one needs to make to solve the problem.
For example, a research method can be broken down into a set of interdependent design decisions, such as, choice of outer-loop optimizer, morphology representation, and fitness evaluation strategy.
These choices can differ within the literature and the interaction between design choices determines method performance.
Additionally, such surveys often do not find a place in the taxonomy for some specific methods, and therefore enmesh them into the `others' category.  
Separately, there is a lack of actionable open questions, leaving a researcher knowing that gaps exist but not how to begin closing them.
